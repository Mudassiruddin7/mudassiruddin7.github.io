About Me

I am a researcher, builder and founder from Hyderabad, India. I completed my B.E. in Computer Science and Engineering in 2026 at Muffakham Jah College of Engineering and Technology, affiliated to Osmania University. I started billing clients for code at 17, during my diploma years. Since October 2026 I have worked as a Python Developer – ML Engineer at AI Logic Neural Network, on encoder-decoder translation models.

My work sits on three questions. What is a model actually computing? That is the circuit extraction work: pulling the small subgraph responsible for one behaviour out of a language model without searching the whole graph, and auditing whether the subgraph really carries the behaviour. What survives contact with real hardware? That is the compression and edge work: a disease detector small enough for a Raspberry Pi, an agent that pays for full precision only on the steps that need it. Does the system do what it claims? That is the verification work: whether a sepsis alert's explanation is true to the lab value it cites, and whether a multi-agent workflow still follows the instruction it started from.

First-author papers: circuit extraction in language models, disease-aware pruning that runs on a Raspberry Pi (under review at JMLR), and a pre-registered study of sepsis-alert explanations across 121 hospitals with Prof. Hamdi Al-Jamimi (manuscript). I also send fixes to open-source libraries such as TransformerLens, nnsight and HEPData, and I write up what I build and what breaks on my blog. If you work on interpretability, efficient deployment or verifying model behaviour, write to me at mohd.mudassiruddin7@gmail.com, or download my CV.

Research record: 4 papers · 4 citations · h-index 1 on Google Scholar.

I don’t build to ship. I build to understand. The shipping is just proof I understood.

Research Experience

  • 2026 – Present Research Intern, Dr. Tasneem Bano Rehman, MJCET – IGSTC WISER Indo–German Government-Funded Project: “AI-Enabled Scout Drone for Real-Time Pest Detection and Geotagging in Precision Agriculture.” Contributing to data acquisition and working with multimodal data (RGB, thermal, LiDAR), with a particular focus on developing and exploring data and sensor fusion approaches for integrating information from multiple sensing modalities, so the project can geotag the parts of a field most affected by pests. With Dr. Rehman, Marina Tropmann-Frick (Hamburg University of Applied Sciences) and Mohammed Arshad, I co-wrote A PRISMA 2020 Systematic Review of Deep Learning Approaches for Paddy and Rice Disease Detection and Classification (manuscript): 1,471 records from seven sources, 112 full texts assessed, 49 studies appraised for dataset quality and reproducibility. Reported accuracies of 86.29–100% come from datasets and protocols that cannot be compared, and few studies release code. In progress: real-time person detection and tracking on a static Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, at 6.1 ms per 50 ms frame on a CPU, with 95% of boxes kept when noise at the sensor's own specification is added.

  • 2026 – Present Visiting Research Collaborator, Prof. Hamdi Al-Jamimi, KFUPM — Do Sepsis Alerts Cite the Lab Value or the Lab Order? (manuscript). Sepsis alert tools show clinicians which findings drove an alert, but a lab result carries two signals: its value and the decision to order it. A per-alert verifier edits each cited lab twice, once on its value and once on its measurement record, and counts the citation as faithful only if normalising the value lowers the risk. Under a pre-registered protocol it audited 45,246 alerts from a model trained at one hospital and applied to 121 hospitals in PhysioNet 2019, MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD. Lactate citations reflected the lactate value in only 20–42% of alerts, and 98–99% of the rest were driven by the measurement record. Removing measurement features raised value-faithful alerts from 63.1% to 95.4% at an AUROC cost of 0.013. Contrary to the pre-registered hypothesis, faithfulness did not fall at new hospitals even as AUROC fell from 0.846 to 0.732.

  • 2024 – Present Research Collaborator, Dr. Fahmina Taranum, MJCET — research collaborator on the Amazon Research Awards 2026 grant (AWS credits) for Trainium-Accelerated Intent Fidelity Checks for Multi-Agent Workflows: a gate that checks each planned tool call in a multi-agent chain against the root instruction, projected into the agent role's action space, and blocks it before it runs, built as a fused NKI kernel on AWS Trainium. Co-author of the stride-4 YOLOv10 study on drone imagery.

  • 2025 – 2026 Undergraduate Research, Dr. Tasneem Bano Rehman, MJCET — co-authored Meta-Learning Guided Pruning for Few-Shot Plant Pathology on Edge Devices (under review at JMLR). Dr. Rehman supervised the edge-deployment and few-shot methodology and contributed to the Raspberry Pi 4 experimental validation.

Research Grants and Proposals

  • 2026 Amazon Research Awards (awarded, AWS credits) — Trainium-Accelerated Intent Fidelity Checks for Multi-Agent Workflows. In a multi-agent chain, a constraint in the user's instruction can be dropped or rewritten at an intermediate hop, and today's safeguards notice only after the tool has run. The proposal puts a fidelity gate before each tool call: it projects the root instruction into the acting agent's role subspace, scores the planned action against it, and blocks unsafe calls before they fire, as a fused NKI kernel on AWS Trainium, with an evaluation harness for AutoGen, ReAct and Bedrock Agents. PI Dr. Fahmina Taranum; I am the research collaborator.

  • 2026 Indian Council of Social Science Research (ICSSR) (under review) — minor research project, ₹15 lakh budget, PI Dr. Fahmina Taranum.

Open Source

Each entry is one library: what was wrong, what I changed, and where the pull requests stand. Every fix comes with a test that fails on the old code (all pull requests).

  • 2026.10 TransformerLens · 6 merged — In the TransformerBridge, cached hooks kept or dropped the batch dimension depending on the code path, alias names were missing from get_caching_hooks, and a hook that returned None crashed run_with_hooks (#1862, #1864, #1865). I then wrote a parity check that compares the bridge with Hugging Face logits on tiny random models of each architecture. It found three more bugs, fixed in #1872–#1874: HunYuan applied QK-norm before RoPE, Cohere and Cohere2 used the wrong RoPE convention, and MPT never updated its KV cache, so cached generation drifted away from Hugging Face.

  • 2026.10 nnsight · merged — The text-generation pipeline left-padded encoder-decoder models, which shifted the logits of the short rows in a batch. Inputs are now padded on the right.

  • 2026.10 HEPData · 3 merged — In hepdata-validator, a data file must now end in .yaml or .json (#71), and every bin is checked for low ≤ value ≤ high on the original numbers, so integers above 253 stay exact (#72). In hepdata-converter, the YAML parser reads only the requested table (#71).

  • 2026.10 scikit-hep and zfit · hepstats merged, the rest in review — hepstats no longer profile-fits toys when no parameter is floating. In zfit, RegularBinning failed on size-one arrays under NumPy 2.4 (42 failing tests) and copying a PDF dropped its observable space. In cabinetry, fits with every parameter fixed crashed, and non-positive symmetrized normalisation effects were accepted.

  • 2026.10 skpro · 5 open — Scalar iloc and parameters broadcast over rows or columns crashed (found by property tests over all 62 distributions), Hurdle reported a variance of 3 where the true value was 25.5, and the energy of NormalMixture ignored the component means. skpro is one of the GC.OS projects in Google Summer of Code, so it is where I started for the 2027 round.

  • 2026.10 Flower, Hugging Face datasets, movement, BrainGlobe and Invenio · open — In Flower, adaptive clipping moved the clipping norm away from its target quantile. In datasets, Dataset.shard raised on empty shards. In movement, cart2pol turned x-y-z data into polar coordinates that matched neither the plane nor the point. In BrainGlobe, interrupting an atlas download with Ctrl+C left an atlas that could not be opened or downloaded again. In invenio-records, a migration failed when a versioning table did not exist.

  • 2026.03 pyGAM · 4 open — p-values and effective degrees of freedom computed with Wood's (2013) rank-r truncated eigendecomposition, overflow in link functions and wrong weights in distributions, and restoring the scikit-learn estimator contract.

  • 2026.02 pgmpy · 11 pull requests, three still open — fixes to the Python library for probabilistic graphical models and causal inference: a rewrite of minimal_dseparator on the moralized ancestral graph, state names lost on fit, keyword arguments dropped by query, a NaN average treatment effect on empty adjustment sets, ApproxInference building its state space from samples instead of the model, and UAI round-trips losing variable names.
