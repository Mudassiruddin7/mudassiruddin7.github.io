About Me

I am a researcher, builder, and founder from Hyderabad, India, completing my B.E. in Computer Science and Engineering (class of 2026) at Muffakham Jah College of Engineering and Technology, affiliated to Osmania University. I started billing clients for code at 17, during my diploma years, and I have been shipping research and products ever since.

My work sits on three questions. What is a model actually computing? That is the circuit extraction work: pulling out the small subgraph inside a billion-parameter network that is responsible for one specific behaviour, without searching the whole graph. What survives contact with real hardware? That is the compression and edge work: a disease detector small enough for a Raspberry Pi, an agent that pays for full precision only on the steps that need it. Does the system do what it claims? That is the verification work: testing whether a hospital model's explanation predicts how the model actually behaves, whether an agent's actions still match the instruction it was given, whether a federated system's fairness can be checked without anyone handing over their data.

Eight of those papers went up on arXiv in 2026, two of them first-author: circuit extraction in billion-parameter language models and disease-aware pruning that runs on a Raspberry Pi. You can download my Resume here.

I contributed to Google Summer of Code 2026 and German Summer of Code 2026, and built at Agentathon 2025, the Guinness World Record for the largest agentic AI hackathon. If you work on interpretability, efficient deployment, or verifying model behaviour, write to me at mohd.mudassiruddin7@gmail.com.

The question underneath all of it is the same one: what is the smallest system that actually solves the problem, and how do I know it is doing what I think it is doing? That constraint is what pushed me from inference on $20 hardware to circuit-level attribution. I do not trust a system from the outside, whether it is a language model, a navigation pipeline or a clinical decision tool.

Research record: 9 papers · 7 citations · h-index 1 — all in 2026.

I don’t build to ship. I build to understand. The shipping is just proof I understood.

Research

Research Experience

  • 2026 – Present Visiting Research Collaborator, Prof. Hamdi Al-Jamimi, KFUPM — Verifying Glass-Box Explanations in Sepsis Clinical Decision Support. Hospitals deploy AI that recommends a sepsis treatment along with an explanation of why. This project tests whether those explanations are faithful: a piece of evidence named in the explanation is perturbed, and the recommendation is checked for the shift the explanation predicted. A recommendation that does not respond the way its own explanation claims is flagged as unreliable, however accurate the underlying prediction is. Cases are labelled by Sepsis-3 consensus criteria and validated on MIMIC-IV and the PhysioNet 2019 Sepsis Challenge, then on HiRID, a Swiss ICU cohort, to test whether faithfulness holds outside US hospital systems — a cross-institutional question almost no existing sepsis AI work has asked.

  • 2024 – 2026 Undergraduate Research, Dr. Fahmina Taranum, MJCET — LLM agent efficiency and TPU-native compute architectures. Co-developed the AetherState and CausalGenome-JAX proposals submitted to the Google TPU Research Awards, with Dr. Taranum as Principal Investigator on both.

  • 2025 – 2026 Undergraduate Research, Dr. Tasneem Bano Rehman, MJCET — co-authored Meta-Learning Guided Pruning for Few-Shot Plant Pathology on Edge Devices (under review at JMLR). Research Funding Proposals

Four competitive proposals submitted in 2026, all under review, each with Dr. Fahmina Taranum (MJCET) as Principal Investigator.

  • 2026 Google TPU Research Awards — AetherState: TPU-Native Evidence Memory and KV Cache Control for Long-Horizon Agents. Long agent runs accumulate context with nothing linking cached KV state to the evidence it encodes: a summary drifts from its source span, a KV page survives because it is cheap to reuse long after its evidence went stale, and retrieval identity sits separate from cache identity, forcing repeated prefill. The proposal makes memory a first-class object owning its source-span hash, provenance chain, retention policy and KV page metadata, with Pallas kernels and a Tunix-style controller managing the lifecycle on TPU. Target venue: MLSys 2027 · 70–100k TPU chip-hours, 20 TB storage.

  • 2026 Google TPU Research Awards — CausalGenome-JAX: Perturbation-Grounded Regulatory Genome Modeling Under Leakage-Resistant Evaluation. Regulatory genomic models are evaluated under splits that share chromosome, cell-type and assay-lab structure, letting correlation stand in for causation. A model can score high correlation on chromatin and RNA tracks and still fail on the disease variant that matters, because the split let it reuse nearby sequence. The proposal freezes four orthogonal holdouts before training, then tests whether MPRA and Perturb-seq supervision improves noncoding variant-effect prediction beyond correlation-only baselines. Target venue: NeurIPS or ICML 2027 · 180–260k TPU chip-hours, 50 TB storage.

  • 2026 GARA, Early Fault-Tolerant Algorithms and Applications — Circuit-Level Cost and Industrial Viability of Locally-Quantum Decoders for Decoded Quantum Interferometry, as Co-Principal Investigator with Dr. Fahmina Taranum as PI. Decoded Quantum Interferometry turns sparse combinatorial optimisation into LDPC decoding, so whether it beats classical computing comes down to the decoder. A 2026 locally-quantum decoder outperforms belief propagation at many (k, D) settings, but it has never been built as a circuit, never been costed under the FLASQ surface-code model, and nobody has checked whether real industrial problems even land in the region where it wins. This project builds the decoder in Qualtran, costs it under FLASQ, reruns the industrial ILP-to-XORSAT pipeline on it, and reports where the numbers land — including the case where the answer is that belief propagation stays the right practical choice. Six months, classical compute only, everything released under Apache 2.0.

  • 2026 Amazon Research Awards — Temporal Belief State Auditing for Regulatory Compliance in Multi-Agent AI Systems. A formal framework for tracking what an agent believes about its task state over time, flagging when the accumulated belief state drifts outside the documented authorization scope. Target application: regulated financial and healthcare deployments.

Open Source

  • 2026 Google Summer of Code · pgmpy — contributed causal structure learning utilities and inference pipeline improvements to the Python library for probabilistic graphical models, including structured pruning utilities that resolved memory bottlenecks in sparse Bayesian network inference on large graphs, letting pgmpy handle sparser structures without exceeding available RAM. The work sits inside variable elimination and belief propagation, at the level where memory-bandwidth trade-offs become visible. Merged to main through review with maintainers across Europe and the US; pgmpy is used by research groups working on causal AI, Bayesian modelling and medical decision systems.

  • 2026 German Summer of Code · pyGAM — implemented distributed inference tooling for the generalized additive modelling library, letting it run inference across distributed compute nodes instead of a single machine. European research infrastructure increasingly runs on distributed clusters, and single-machine inference had been blocking pyGAM adoption in several scientific computing contexts; this closes that gap for large-scale scientific datasets.
